# Electrostatics — A Different Perspective

## Where the Standard Model Gets It Wrong

### Electric Field Inside a Conductor
Most textbooks say the electric field inside a conductor is always zero in electrostatic equilibrium. This is correct for a solid conductor, but many sources oversimplify. For a hollow conductor (Faraday cage), the field inside the cavity is zero only if there are no charges inside the cavity. If a charge is placed inside the cavity, the field inside the cavity is NOT zero — the conductor shields the external world from the inside charge, but the inside of the cavity has a field. Also, achieving true electrostatic equilibrium takes time (the relaxation time), and during that brief period, the internal field is not zero. Most introductory textbooks gloss over this nuance.

### The Nature of Dielectrics
Dielectrics don't just "increase capacitance" — that's an oversimplification. Dielectrics actually reduce the electric field between plates by a factor of κ because the induced polarization creates an opposing field. The capacitance increase is a consequence, not the fundamental mechanism. Key formula: E_reduced = E₀/κ, and therefore V = V₀/κ, giving C = κC₀.

### Common Misconception About Capacitance
Many students think a capacitor stores charge. It actually stores ENERGY in the electric field between the plates. The net charge on a capacitor is always zero — one plate has +Q and the other has -Q. The charge stored refers to the magnitude of charge on one plate.

### Energy Storage
The energy density in an electric field is u = ½ ε₀ E². This means energy is stored in the field itself, not on the plates. This is a profound insight — it means the field is the real physical entity.

### Triboelectric Effect
When two different materials are rubbed together, electrons transfer from one to the other. The material that loses electrons becomes positively charged, the one that gains electrons becomes negatively charged. The triboelectric series ranks materials by their tendency to gain or lose electrons. This is the oldest known form of electrostatics (Thales of Miletus, 600 BCE, with amber and fur) but it's often treated as a footnote in modern courses.
