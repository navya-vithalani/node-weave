# Electrostatics — Lecture Notes Summary

## Basic Principles
Electrostatics deals with stationary electric charges and their interactions. Like charges repel, opposite charges attract. The force follows an inverse-square law (Coulomb's law). Charge is quantized and conserved — the total charge in an isolated system remains constant.

## Coulomb's Law
F = (1/4πε₀) * |q1 * q2| / r², where 1/4πε₀ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². The permittivity of free space ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m². This is mathematically analogous to Newton's law of gravitation but with two key differences: electrostatic forces can be attractive or repulsive (gravity is always attractive), and electrostatic forces are much stronger than gravitational forces.

## Electric Fields
The electric field is defined as E = F/q₀. For continuous charge distributions, we integrate: E = ∫ dE = ∫ (k * dq / r²) * r̂. Common field formulas: point charge (E = kq/r²), infinite line charge (E = 2kλ/r), infinite plane (E = σ/2ε₀).

## Electric Potential
V = kq/r for a point charge. Potential difference ΔV = -∫ E·dl. The electron-volt (eV) is a unit of energy: 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Millikan's oil drop experiment measured the elementary charge e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

## Gauss's Law
Φ = ∮ E·dA = Q_enc/ε₀. Applications: field of a spherical shell (E = 0 inside, E = kQ/r² outside), field of an infinite sheet (E = σ/2ε₀ independent of distance), field of a conducting sphere (E = 0 inside, E = kQ/r² outside).

## Conductors
Electrostatic equilibrium: E = 0 inside a conductor, excess charge on the surface, field just outside is perpendicular to the surface with magnitude σ/ε₀. The potential is constant throughout the conductor.

## Capacitors and Energy
Capacitance: C = Q/V. Parallel plate: C = ε₀A/d (without dielectric). Energy stored: U = Q²/2C = CV²/2 = QV/2. Important: The electric field between plates of a parallel plate capacitor is E = σ/ε₀ = V/d, which is uniform in the ideal case.
